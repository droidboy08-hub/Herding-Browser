# Privacy Policy

**Last updated: 4 August 2026**

Herding is a web browser for iPhone. It has no account system, no analytics, and
no advertising. This policy explains what the app does with information, which is
almost nothing.

## What we collect

**Nothing.** The developer of Herding does not collect, receive, store or
transmit any personal data. There is no server, no account, no telemetry, no
crash reporting and no advertising identifier.

## What stays on your device

Herding stores the following locally on your iPhone, so the browser can work.
None of it leaves the device, and none of it is visible to the developer:

- **Browsing history** — pages you visit, their titles and visit times.
- **Open tabs** — their addresses and page state, so they survive a restart.
- **Bookmarks and favourites** — the sites you choose to keep.
- **Downloads** — files you download, in the app's Downloads folder.
- **Settings** — your preferences, including your chosen search engine.
- **Website data** — cookies, caches and local storage belonging to the sites
  you visit, managed by Apple's WebKit exactly as in Safari.

You can delete any of it from within the app. Deleting the app removes all of it.

Private browsing keeps history, tabs and website data in memory only, and
discards them when the session ends.

## What leaves your device, and where it goes

Herding is a browser, so it makes network requests on your behalf. These go to
third parties, never to us:

- **The sites you visit.** Normal web browsing. Those sites see your requests as
  any browser's.
- **Your search engine.** Text you type in the search field is sent to whichever
  engine you have selected. The default is DuckDuckGo. You can change it in
  Settings. That engine's own privacy policy applies.
- **Site icons.** The browser fetches a site's favicon from that same site, to
  show beside its tab. No third-party icon service is used.
- **Filter list updates.** Ad and tracker filter lists are plain text files
  downloaded from their publishers, such as EasyList. These requests contain no
  information about you or your browsing — only a request for the list itself.

## Content blocking

Herding blocks ads and trackers using public filter lists. Most blocking is done
by Apple's WebKit from rules compiled on your device, which means the app itself
never sees the addresses of the resources being blocked.

## Children

Herding gives access to the open web and is not directed at children.
